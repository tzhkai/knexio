About Tower of Hanoi

The Tower of Hanoi is a classic mathematical puzzle invented by French mathematician Édouard Lucas in 1883. It consists of three rods and a number of disks of different sizes which can slide onto any rod.

Mathematical Facts

  • Minimum moves required: 2n − 1 (where n = number of disks)
  • 3 disks → 7 moves  |  4 disks → 15 moves  |  5 disks → 31 moves  |  6 disks → 63 moves
  • If you moved one disk per second, 64 disks would take ~585 billion years!
  • The puzzle is used to teach recursion in computer science courses worldwide.

Strategy Tips

  • Think several moves ahead before acting
  • Use tower B as temporary storage strategically
  • The largest disk should move only once — directly to tower C
  • Smaller disks move far more frequently than larger ones

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I move a disk?

Click a tower to select it (it highlights in blue). Then click the destination tower to move the top disk there. Click the same tower again to deselect.

What does the Auto Solve button do?

It resets the puzzle and demonstrates the optimal recursive solution step by step, so you can watch and learn the algorithm.

How is the minimum number of moves calculated?

The formula is 2n − 1. For 4 disks that's 2⁴ − 1 = 15 moves. Achieving the minimum earns a perfect-solution badge!
