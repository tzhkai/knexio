Discord Mic Not Working? Here's How to Fix It

If your microphone isn't working on Discord, others can't hear you in voice channels or calls. This guide covers every common cause — from incorrect input device selection and OS-level permission denials to hardware conflicts and Discord-specific settings. Solutions work for the Discord desktop app on Windows and Mac, as well as the browser version.
Quick check before anything else: Look at the bottom-left of Discord for the microphone icon. A red diagonal line through it means you're muted. Click it once to unmute. Also check the keyboard shortcut — Ctrl+Shift+M (Windows) or Cmd+Shift+M (Mac) toggles mute instantly and can accidentally silence you.

Introduction: Why Discord Mic Issues Are Common

Discord uses its own audio subsystem and handles microphone input differently from most other applications. It can override your system's default audio device, use advanced noise suppression that sometimes breaks legitimate audio, and operate with a separate permission layer that doesn't always sync with Windows or Mac OS permissions. This means your mic can work perfectly in Zoom, Skype, or Steam calls but fail entirely in Discord — and both apps use the same physical microphone.

Common Causes of Discord Mic Problems

  • Wrong input device selected — Discord may default to your monitor speakers, webcam mic, or Bluetooth headset instead of your actual headset
  • Muted at OS level — Windows and Mac each have their own mute toggle that Discord respects
  • Missing microphone permission — Discord needs explicit permission from your operating system to access the microphone
  • Exclusive mode enabled (Windows) — some audio drivers lock the microphone to a single application
  • Overly aggressive noise suppression — Discord's AI noise cancellation sometimes suppresses your voice entirely
  • USB device briefly disconnecting — USB headsets that momentarily lose connection reset their Discord input settings

Fix: Check Discord's Input Settings First

Step 1 — Open Discord Voice & Video Settings

Click the gear icon (⚙️) next to your username in the bottom-left panel. In the left menu, click Voice & Video. This is where all Discord audio input and output is controlled. Never change audio settings from the system control panel when fixing Discord — Discord has its own independent settings that override the OS defaults.

Step 2 — Select the Correct Input Device

Under "Input Device," click the dropdown and ensure your actual microphone is selected. Common pitfalls: "Default" may pick a different device than you expect, "Monitor" or "Speaker" options in the list aren't microphones, and Bluetooth headsets often appear with a different name than expected. If your headset has a separate USB sound card, select that specific device, not the built-in laptop microphone.

Step 3 — Test the Input Volume

While in Voice & Video settings, speak normally into your microphone. Watch the input volume meter — the bar should pulse green as you speak. If the bar stays gray or flat, Discord isn't receiving your voice. Adjust the "Input Volume" slider upward until the meter responds. Click "Let's Check" to run Discord's built-in mic test, which walks you through speaking and shows whether your voice is being detected.

Step 4 — Switch to Push-to-Talk

If your voice isn't triggering the input meter at all, switch from Voice Activity mode to Push-to-Talk mode. In Voice & Video settings, under "Input Mode," select Push-to-Talk. Assign a key (the default is usually good) and press it while watching the volume meter — the bar should activate when you hold the key, even if it doesn't respond to voice. This tells you whether the issue is with detection sensitivity or the mic itself.

Fix: Grant OS-Level Microphone Permissions

On Windows — Check Privacy Settings

Press Win+I to open Settings → Privacy & Security → scroll down to Microphone → Ensure "Microphone access" is turned on at the top. Below that, scroll to "Allow apps to access your microphone" and ensure it's on. In the list below, find Discord and make sure it's toggled to On. If Discord isn't listed, scroll to the bottom and click "Reset permissions on this page," then restart Discord.

On Mac — Grant Permission in System Settings

Open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone. In the list, find Discord and ensure the checkbox is ticked. If Discord isn't in the list, quit Discord completely (from the menu bar → Discord → Quit), then relaunch it — this often triggers the permission prompt to appear. If the permission is granted but the mic still doesn't work, also check System Settings → Sound → Input to confirm the correct device is selected at the system level.

On Browser — Check Site Permissions

If you're using Discord in a browser (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, or Safari), the browser itself needs permission to access your microphone. When you first join a voice channel, the browser will prompt for microphone access — click Allow. If you accidentally clicked Block, go to browser Settings → Privacy and security → Site settings → Microphone. Find Discord in the list and set it to Allow. In Chrome, you can also click the padlock icon in the address bar → Site permissions → Microphone → Allow.

Fix: Windows-Specific Audio Settings

Step 1 — Disable Exclusive Mode for Your Microphone

Some audio drivers enable "exclusive mode," which lets only one app at a time use the microphone. This can block Discord from accessing it even when other settings are correct. Right-click the speaker icon in the Windows taskbar → Sound → Recording tab → right-click your microphone → Properties → Advanced tab → Under "Exclusive Mode," uncheck both "Allow applications to take exclusive control of this device" and "Give exclusive mode applications priority" → Click Apply → OK.

Step 2 — Set Discord as Default Communication Device

Windows separates audio devices into two categories: playback (speakers) and communication. Discord may be using the wrong category. Right-click the speaker icon → Sound → Recording tab → right-click your microphone → Set as Default Device. Then right-click it again → Set as Default Communication Device. Both should be set to your actual microphone.

Step 3 — Run Discord as Administrator

Some applications with elevated security requirements (games, developer tools) can conflict with Discord's audio access when Discord isn't running with full permissions. Close Discord → right-click the Discord shortcut or exe → Run as administrator. In the Discord login screen, go to the settings gear icon and verify the microphone is working. To always run Discord as admin: right-click the shortcut → Properties → Compatibility tab → check "Run this program as administrator."

Fix: Noise Suppression and Advanced Audio Settings

Step 1 — Disable Krisp Noise Cancellation

Discord's built-in Krisp add-on (offered with Nitro or as a separate subscription) processes your audio to remove background noise. When Krisp malfunctions or conflicts with certain USB headsets, it can cut your microphone entirely. In Discord Voice & Video settings, look for "Krisp" in the left menu (if available) → click "Disable Noise Cancellation". Alternatively, scroll to the bottom of Voice & Video → Advanced → toggle off "Noise Suppression" and "Echo Cancellation". Test your mic after each change.

Step 2 — Check Your Bitrate Setting

In Voice & Video settings → scroll down to "Advanced". Check the "Automatic bandwidth mode" setting — if it's set to "Manual" and you've set a very low custom bitrate (below 48 kbps), your audio quality will be too low for Discord to process it. Reset to "Automatic" for best results. Also ensure "Opus" is selected as the audio codec if given a choice.

Step 3 — Reset Voice Settings to Default

If you've made many changes trying to fix the issue, you may have inadvertently made things worse. At the bottom of Voice & Video settings, click "Reset Voice Settings". This returns all Discord audio settings to their defaults without affecting your server or account. After resetting, configure only the input device and try again.

Fix: Hardware and USB Issues

For USB Headsets

USB headsets have their own internal sound processing, and Windows or Discord may not always handle the handoff correctly. Unplug the USB headset, wait 10 seconds, and plug it back into a different USB port (preferably a USB 2.0 port for maximum compatibility, not USB-C unless your headset specifically uses it). In Discord, reselect the headset from the input device dropdown. If the headset has a physical mute button on the ear cup, make sure it isn't pressed.

For Bluetooth Headsets

Bluetooth headsets often have significant audio latency and can disconnect momentarily during operation. In Windows Settings → Bluetooth & devices → check that Discord isn't set to use a Hands-Free profile for voice (which has very low quality). Remove and re-pair the Bluetooth device from Windows Settings. In Discord, also check that the selected input device matches the Bluetooth headset's name exactly — Windows sometimes creates multiple audio device entries for one headset.

Fix: Specific Discord Error Messages

"Microphone not detected" in Discord

This means Discord can't see any microphone connected to your system. Check that the microphone is visible in Windows Sound settings (Control Panel → Sound → Recording tab). If it's visible there but not in Discord, try toggling Discord off and on again. If it's not visible anywhere, the issue is with the microphone itself — test it in another application like Voice Memos (Mac) or Windows Voice Recorder.

Mic cuts out every few seconds

This is almost always a USB connection issue with headsets or an overaggressive Push-to-Talk key binding. If using a wired USB headset, try a different USB port. If using a Bluetooth headset, disable Bluetooth power saving mode in Windows Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Device settings → your headset → Additional Bluetooth settings → untick "Allow Bluetooth devices to turn off this device." Also check that Discord's Push-to-Talk key isn't accidentally bound to a key that frequently gets pressed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why can no one hear me on Discord?

Most commonly: wrong input device selected, mic muted at Discord or OS level, or Windows/Mac microphone permission denied. Check the Discord mic icon in the bottom-left first (red line = muted). Then open Discord Voice & Video settings and confirm the correct device is selected in the Input Device dropdown.

Why does my mic cut out during calls?

This is usually caused by Discord's noise suppression incorrectly identifying your voice as noise, or a USB device momentarily losing its connection. Disable Krisp/Noise Suppression in Voice & Video → Advanced settings. If using a USB headset, try a different USB port. For Bluetooth headsets, make sure the headset isn't entering power-save mode mid-call.

Does Discord mic work in browser vs the desktop app?

Yes, but the browser version requires microphone permission from the browser itself in addition to your OS permissions. When you first join a voice channel in the browser, grant permission when prompted. If you accidentally denied it, go to browser Settings → Site Permissions → Microphone → find Discord → Allow.

Why does my mic work in other apps but not Discord?

Discord sometimes uses a different audio API than other apps, particularly on Windows. Try switching Input Mode from Voice Activity to Push-to-Talk in Discord Voice & Video settings. Also check that Windows hasn't set Discord's audio to Hands-Free mode (which has very limited quality) — right-click your microphone in Sound settings → Properties → Advanced tab → ensure the format is set to "16 bit, 48000 Hz (Studio Quality)."

My headset mic works in games but not in Discord — why?

Many gaming headsets with USB connections present themselves as a separate audio device for gaming software and a different one for standard applications. Open Discord Voice & Video settings → Input Device dropdown, and scroll through all available options to find the one that corresponds to your headset specifically (it may be named differently than the gaming software's label). The "Default" option often doesn't pick the right one.

When to Contact Discord Support

If you've worked through all steps above and Discord still can't detect or use your microphone, the issue may be with Discord's backend servers or your account status. Contact Discord support at dis.gd/contact if you have an active Discord Nitro subscription and still experience issues, your account has been flagged for suspicious activity which can sometimes affect voice features, the Discord app is crashing specifically when you try to access voice settings (which can indicate a corrupted installation), or you are experiencing this issue across multiple devices simultaneously (suggesting a server-side issue rather than a device problem).
