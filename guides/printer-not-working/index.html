Printer Not Working? Here's How to Fix It

Whether your printer shows as offline, won't print at all, or is printing blank pages, this guide covers every common scenario. From USB and Wi-Fi connection issues to ink problems and driver errors, you'll find step-by-step solutions for Windows, Mac, and all major printer brands including HP, Canon, Epson, and Brother.
Most common fix: Turn the printer off, wait 30 seconds, turn it back on, and restart your computer. This resolves approximately 40% of all printer issues immediately. Always start here before trying more involved steps.

Introduction: Common Printer Problems

Printer issues typically fall into one of four categories: the printer is detected but shows offline, it won't print at all, it prints but output is poor quality, or it isn't being detected by the computer at all. Identifying which category you're in helps you find the right fix quickly. Most problems stem from a small number of root causes — once you know what to look for, the solution is usually straightforward.

Common Causes of Printer Problems

  • Print Spooler service crashed — Windows's print service gets stuck frequently, leaving jobs queued and marking the printer offline
  • Outdated or corrupted printer driver — Drivers age out after Windows updates, or become corrupted over time
  • Wi-Fi connection dropped — Wi-Fi printers lose their network connection, especially after router restarts or sleep mode
  • USB cable or port failure — USB cables degrade; ports can fail, particularly on laptops
  • Low or empty ink/toner cartridges — Many printers refuse to print or produce blank output when ink is depleted
  • Paper jam or misfeeding — Even small scraps of paper in the feed mechanism cause errors and halts
  • Print queue backed up — A single failed print job can block the entire queue

Fix: Printer Shows as Offline (Windows)

Step 1 — Verify Physical Connection

Before touching software settings, confirm the printer is actually powered on and connected. Check that the USB cable is firmly seated (or that the Wi-Fi indicator on the printer shows a solid connection). If you're using a network printer, print a configuration page from the printer's control panel — this page will show the current IP address and network status.

Step 2 — Clear the Print Queue

Many offline errors are caused by a stuck print job blocking new ones. Press Win+R → type printmanagement.msc → press Enter. Click "All Printers" in the left panel. In the right panel, double-click your printer → click "See what's printing." In the new window, go to the Printer menu → Cancel All Documents. If documents are stuck, right-click each one individually and select Cancel.

Step 3 — Restart the Print Spooler Service

The Print Spooler is a Windows background service that manages all print jobs. When it crashes, the entire print system stops working. Press Win+R → type services.msc → press Enter. Find Print Spooler in the list → right-click → Properties → click Stop. Wait 5 seconds, then right-click again → Start. Now try printing again.

Step 4 — Set Printer as Default

Open Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Printers & scanners. Select your printer → click Manage → Set as default. Also click Printing preferences and uncheck "Let Windows manage my default printer." When Windows auto-selects a disconnected network printer as default, it causes persistent offline errors.

Step 5 — Run the Windows Printer Troubleshooter

Open Settings → System → Troubleshoot → Other troubleshooters → Printer → Run. Windows will scan for common issues including driver problems, spooler errors, and permission issues. Follow any recommendations the troubleshooter provides.

Fix: Printer Not Detected

For USB Printers on Windows

Try a different USB cable first — USB cables are the most common failure point. If that doesn't work, try a different USB port (directly on the computer, not through a hub). Open Device Manager → Universal Serial Bus controllers → look for any device with a yellow warning icon. Right-click it → Uninstall device → disconnect the USB cable → restart your computer → reconnect the cable.

For Wi-Fi Printers on Windows

Make sure your computer and printer are on the same Wi-Fi network. Print a network configuration page from the printer menu (usually under Settings → Network or WLAN). Note the IP address shown. Then go to Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Printers & scanners → Add device. If the printer doesn't appear, click "Add manually" and enter its IP address. Restart your router if the printer shows "Connection error" — router restarts often resolve Wi-Fi printer disconnections.

For Network/Shared Printers

If the printer is connected to another computer on your network (not directly to yours), open Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Printers & scanners → Add device. Windows will search the network. If the printer isn't found, go to the host computer, open Printer Properties → Sharing tab, ensure "Share this printer" is checked, and note the share name. On your computer, go to the same Add device screen → "The printer that I want isn't listed" → select "Select a shared printer by name" → type \\COMPUTERNAME\PRINTERSHARENAME.

Fix: Printer Not Printing (All Platforms)

Step 1 — Check Ink and Toner Levels

Open the printer's software on your computer (search for your printer brand in the Start menu, e.g., "HP Smart" or "Canon IJ"). Check the ink/toner status from the dashboard. On most inkjet printers, even one empty color cartridge can prevent all printing. For laser printers, a low toner warning often still allows printing but with poor quality. Replace cartridges proactively — don't wait for empty.

Step 2 — Check Paper and Paper Path

Remove all paper from the tray. Inspect the feed roller area for small torn pieces of paper — these jams are often invisible and cause the printer to think it's jammed. Fan the paper stack and reload it squarely, making sure it doesn't exceed the tray's maximum fill line. For rear-loading printers, check the back paper path as well.

Step 3 — Run the Print Head Cleaning Cycle

If your printed pages have streaks, gaps, or missing colors, the print head nozzles are clogged. Open the printer software → find Maintenance or Utilities tab → run Print Head Cleaning. After cleaning, print a nozzle check pattern to confirm all lines are solid. Repeat the cleaning cycle up to three times if needed. For heavily clogged heads, some printers have a "Deep Cleaning" option that uses more ink but is more effective.

Step 4 — Reinstall the Printer Driver

Drivers are the most common cause of printers that won't print at all. Go to Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Printers & scanners → select your printer → Remove device. Now go to your printer manufacturer's website (hp.com/support, support.epson.com, support.brother.com, or support.canonglobal.com) → enter your exact model number → download the latest driver for your OS version. Run the installer and follow the prompts.

Fix: Mac Printer Issues

Step 1 — Reset the Printing System

On Mac, resetting the printing system clears all printer configurations and forces macOS to rediscover and reinstall printers cleanly. Go to System Settings → Printers & Scanners. Right-click (or Control+click) anywhere in the printer list on the left side → select Reset printing system. Confirm when prompted. Your printers will be removed. Then click the + button to re-add your printer. macOS will automatically download and install the correct driver from Apple servers.

Step 2 — Check System Preferences Lock

If you're prompted for a username and password when trying to add a printer, this means your Mac's printing system is locked. Enter your Mac administrator username and password to proceed. This is a security feature — only admin accounts can modify printers on macOS.

Step 3 — Manually Add a Network Printer on Mac

Go to System Settings → Printers & Scanners → +. Click the IP tab. Enter the printer's IP address (from the printer's network configuration page), set the Protocol to Line Printer Daemon (LPD) or IPP, and enter the queue name if needed. macOS will attempt to detect the correct driver. If it fails, search for your printer model on the manufacturer's site and download the Mac driver, then use the Software tab in the Add Printer dialog to install it.

Fix: Specific Printer Error Messages

"Printer Offline" on Mac

If a printer shows as offline on Mac despite being connected, go to System Settings → Printers & Scanners. Select the printer → look for the "Queue" or "Job" list. If there are stuck jobs, select each one and click the trash icon to delete. Then go to the printer's control panel on the physical device and cancel any blinking error lights. Finally, click Options & Supplies → Utility → Open Printer Utility → run a test page.

"Ink Cartridge Not Recognized"

This error occurs when the printer can't read the chip on the cartridge. Remove the cartridge and clean the gold contacts with a soft, lint-free cloth slightly dampened with water. Reinsert it firmly. If it still isn't recognized, try a different cartridge from a new sealed pack. Third-party refilled cartridges sometimes fail to register because the chip is damaged or the ink level isn't registering correctly.

"0x8007000e" or "Not Enough Memory" Error

These errors mean the printer's buffer is full, usually from a very large or complex print job. Cancel the current print job, power cycle the printer, and try a lower-resolution version of the document. For PDF printing issues, try printing the PDF as an image (in the print dialog, check "Print as image" under Advanced settings).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my printer keep going offline?

If a printer repeatedly goes offline, it's usually a network issue (Wi-Fi printers) or the Print Spooler crashing repeatedly. Assign a static IP address to your Wi-Fi printer via the printer's network settings menu — this prevents it from getting a different IP after router restarts, which is a common cause of recurring offline status.

Why is my printer printing blank pages?

Blank pages are almost always caused by empty or clogged ink cartridges, an unseated print head, or the wrong paper type selected in print settings. Run the printer's nozzle check (found in the Maintenance/Utilities section of the printer software) to see if all color lines print correctly. If lines are missing, run the head cleaning cycle. If all lines appear but pages are still blank, replace the ink cartridges.

How do I reinstall my printer driver on Windows?

Go to Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Printers & scanners → select your printer → Remove device. Then visit the manufacturer's support website (HP at hp.com/support, Canon at support.canonglobal.com, Epson at epson.com/support, or Brother at support.brother.com). Enter your exact model number, select your Windows version, and download the latest driver. Run the installer as Administrator for best results.

Why is my printer printing streaks or faded text?

Streaking and faded output indicate a clogged print head or low ink in one color channel. Use the printer's built-in Cleaning Cycles (accessible through the printer's control panel menu or the printer software on your computer) to clear clogged nozzles. After running the cycle, print a nozzle check pattern to verify the fix. If the problem persists after two cleaning cycles, the print head may need physical replacement.

My printer was working yesterday but not today — what changed?

Common overnight changes include a Windows update replacing the driver, your router restarting and changing the printer's IP address (Wi-Fi printers), or the Print Spooler service crashing. Check Windows Update history for recent updates, verify the printer's network status, and restart the Print Spooler service as described above.

When to Contact Printer Support

If you've worked through all the steps above and your printer still won't work, the issue may be hardware-related. Contact your printer manufacturer's support if you see any of these signs: the printer makes grinding or clicking sounds and won't feed paper (mechanical failure), the printer powers on but the screen stays blank (display or logic board issue), the printer loses power randomly during printing (power supply or internal component failure), or you've tried multiple USB ports and cables with no detection at all (USB port on the printer may be failed).

Before contacting support, note your printer's exact model number and serial number (found on a label on the back or bottom of the printer), describe exactly what happens when you try to print (any error messages or lights), and note any recent changes to your computer or network.
