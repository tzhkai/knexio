How to Take a Screenshot

Quick shortcuts for every device and OS

Why Screenshot? Screenshots capture exactly what's on your screen, perfect for sharing error messages, saving online receipts, capturing conversations, creating tutorials, and documenting issues for tech support.

Windows

Windows offers multiple screenshot methods depending on what you need to capture. From quick clipboard captures to saved files, Windows has you covered.

Windows 10 and 11 Screenshot Shortcuts

Win + Shift + S
Opens Snipping Tool — select area with mouse
Win + PrtScn
Full screen → saves to Pictures/Screenshots
PrtScn
Full screen to clipboard (paste with Ctrl+V)
Alt + PrtScn
Active window only → to clipboard

Using Snipping Tool (Windows 10/11)

The Snipping Tool is built into Windows and offers more flexibility than keyboard shortcuts:

  1. Press Win + Shift + S or search for "Snipping Tool" in the Start menu
  2. Choose your capture mode: Rectangle, Freeform, Window, or Full-screen
  3. Click and drag to select the area (or click a window for Window mode)
  4. The screenshot opens in the Snipping Tool editor
  5. Click "Save" to save or "Copy" to copy to clipboard
💡 Tip: In Windows 11, Snipping Tool has annotation tools — use the pen or highlighter to mark up screenshots before saving.

Using Snip & Sketch (Windows 10)

Windows 10 also includes Snip & Sketch:

  1. Press Win + Shift + S
  2. The screen dims, and a toolbar appears at the top
  3. Select your capture area

Where Screenshots are Saved

By default, Windows saves screenshots to:

  • Windows 11: This PC → Pictures → Screenshots
  • Windows 10: This PC → Pictures → Screenshots
  • Or: Press Win + E → Navigate to Pictures → Screenshots

Mac

Mac offers elegant screenshot capabilities with customizable options and easy saving.

macOS Screenshot Shortcuts

Cmd + Shift + 3
Full screen screenshot → saves to Desktop
Cmd + Shift + 4
Select area to screenshot → saves to Desktop
Cmd + Shift + 4 + Space
Screenshot a specific window (cursor changes)
Cmd + Shift + 5
Screenshot toolbar (all options) — macOS Mojave+

Using Screenshot Toolbar (macOS Mojave and later)

Press Cmd + Shift + 5 to open the screenshot toolbar with options:

  • Capture Entire Screen — Click the screen or press Space then click
  • Capture Selected Window — Click the window you want
  • Capture Selected Portion — Click and drag to select area
  • Options: Save to Desktop, Documents, Clipboard, or Preview
  • Timer: 5 or 10 second delay option

Mac Screenshot to Clipboard

Want to paste directly into a document or app? Add Control to any shortcut:

  • Control + Cmd + Shift + 3 — Full screen to clipboard
  • Control + Cmd + Shift + 4 — Selected area to clipboard

Then press Cmd + V to paste anywhere.

Where Mac Screenshots are Saved

By default, Mac saves screenshots to:

  • Desktop — Most common location
  • Documents — If you change the default in Screenshot toolbar
  • Clipboard — If using Control key modifier
💡 Change Default Location: Open Screenshot toolbar (Cmd+Shift+5) → Click "Options" → Choose where to save.

iPhone

iPhone screenshot methods vary by model. iPhones with Face ID and those with Home buttons have different button combinations.

iPhone with Face ID (X, XS, XR, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16)

  • Full screenshot: Press and hold the Side button + Volume Up simultaneously
  • The screen flashes white, and you hear a camera shutter sound
  • Screenshot appears in the bottom-left corner — tap to edit or swipe left to dismiss
💡 Quick Edit: Tap the thumbnail to mark up, crop, or add text before saving.

iPhone with Home Button (SE, 8, 7, 6, etc.)

  • Full screenshot: Press and hold the Side/Top button + Home button simultaneously
  • Press both buttons at the same time and release quickly

iPad Screenshot Methods

  • iPad with Face ID: Press and hold Top button + Volume Up (or Volume Down)
  • iPad with Home button: Press and hold Top button + Home button

Where iPhone Screenshots are Saved

  • Photos app: Open Photos → Recents or Albums → Screenshots
  • Files app: Go to On My iPhone → Screenshots

Taking Long Screenshot (Scroll Capture)

On iPhone, you can capture entire webpages or documents that scroll:

  1. Take a normal screenshot
  2. Tap the thumbnail preview
  3. Tap "Full Page" at the top (if available)
  4. Adjust the capture area if needed
  5. Tap "Done" to save
💡 Note: Long screenshots only work in Safari and certain apps that support scrolling capture.

Android

Android screenshot methods vary by device manufacturer, but the most common methods work on most phones.

Standard Android Screenshot

  • Press and hold Power + Volume Down simultaneously for 1-2 seconds
  • The screen flashes, and you hear a shutter sound (if not muted)

Android Screenshot Variations

Power + Volume Down
Standard screenshot on most Android phones
Palm swipe
Swipe edge of hand across screen (Samsung, some others)
3-finger swipe
Some Android devices support three-finger swipe down
Bixby/Google Assistant
Say "Take a screenshot" or use power button menu

Samsung Galaxy Devices

Samsung phones often have additional screenshot features:

  • Palm swipe: Settings → Advanced Features → Motion and Gestures → Enable Palm swipe to capture
  • Smart capture: Captures scrolling content automatically
  • Screenshot toolbar: Appears after taking screenshot — includes scroll capture, share, and edit options

Where Android Screenshots are Saved

  • Gallery: Open Gallery → Albums → Screenshots
  • Google Photos: Open Google Photos → Library → Screenshots
  • Files app: Internal Storage → Pictures → Screenshots

Chromebook

Chromebooks use a different key layout for screenshots.

Chromebook Screenshot Shortcuts

Ctrl + Show Windows
Full screen screenshot — Show Windows is F5
Ctrl + Shift + Show Windows
Select area screenshot
💡 Note: The "Show Windows" key is usually the F5 key or the key between Fullscreen and Brightness Down (often has a grid icon).

Where Chromebook Screenshots are Saved

  • Screenshots are saved to the Downloads folder
  • Access via the Files app
  • Or go to Downloads in the file manager

Special Screenshot Scenarios

How to Screenshot a Scrolling Page

Sometimes you need to capture more than fits on one screen. Here are your options:

Browser Extensions (Chrome/Firefox):

  • GoFullPage: Popular Chrome extension that stitches scrolling pages into one image
  • Fireshot: Capture full page as image or PDF

Built-in Methods:

  • Samsung Galaxy: Take screenshot → Tap scroll icon → Keep scrolling → Tap done
  • OnePlus: Take screenshot → Tap "Scroll" icon
  • Google Pixel: Take screenshot → Tap "Capture more" if available

How to Screenshot with Transparent Background

For overlaying screenshots on other content:

On Mac:

  • Use Cmd + Shift + 4 then press and hold Option while selecting a window

On Windows:

  • Use Snipping Tool → Select "Window" mode → Save as PNG

How to Screenshot on Multiple Monitors

On Windows:

  • Win + PrtScn captures all monitors combined
  • Win + Shift + S lets you select which monitor to capture

On Mac:

  • Use Cmd + Shift + 3 to capture all screens
  • Use Cmd + Shift + 4 then select specific monitor

Editing Screenshots

Windows Screenshot Editor

After capturing with Snipping Tool or Snip & Sketch:

  • Use the toolbar to draw, highlight, or add text
  • Crop the image using the crop tool
  • Save as PNG, JPG, or copy to clipboard

Mac Screenshot Editor

After taking a screenshot, click the thumbnail preview:

  • Use the markup toolbar to draw, highlight, or add shapes
  • Add text with the text tool
  • Crop and rotate
  • Save or share directly from the editor

Mobile Screenshot Editing

On iPhone:

  • Tap the thumbnail after capturing
  • Use the markup tools to draw, add text, or crop
  • Tap "Done" to save or share

On Android:

  • Most Android phones show editing options immediately after screenshot
  • Use built-in editors or download from Play Store

Frequently Asked Questions

Where are screenshots saved?

Windows: Pictures/Screenshots folder. Mac: Desktop by default. iPhone/Android: Photos/Gallery app. Chromebook: Downloads folder.

How do I screenshot a scrolling page?

Use browser extensions like GoFullPage (Chrome) or Fireshot. On Samsung Galaxy, use the built-in scroll capture feature after taking a screenshot. You can also use our Website Screenshot tool for capturing entire webpages.

Why isn't my screenshot shortcut working?

On Windows, make sure you're not in a field that requires keyboard input. Try clicking the desktop first. On Mac, check System Preferences → Keyboard → Shortcuts to ensure screenshot shortcuts are enabled. On mobile, make sure both buttons are pressed simultaneously.

Can I screenshot a video while playing?

Yes, the same screenshot shortcuts work while videos are playing. On Windows, use Win + Shift + S for selective capture. On Mac, use the window capture mode. On mobile, simply take a screenshot the normal way while the video is paused or playing.

How do I take a screenshot of just one window?

Windows: Use Alt + PrtScn for the active window, or Win + Shift + S → Window mode. Mac: Press Cmd + Shift + 4 then press Space and click the window.

What's the difference between PrtScn and Win+PrtScn?

PrtScn copies the screenshot to clipboard (paste with Ctrl+V). Win+PrtScn saves the screenshot directly to your Pictures/Screenshots folder as a PNG file.

How do I screenshot on Linux?

Most Linux distributions include screenshot tools: PrtScn usually opens the screenshot tool. Alt + PrtScn captures active window. Shift + PrtScn captures selected area. Check your distro's screenshot app for more options.

Summary

Screenshot methods vary by device and operating system, but the core concept is the same: capture your screen to share, document, or troubleshoot. Windows users have the Snipping Tool, Mac users have the Screenshot toolbar, and mobile users have built-in capture buttons.

Remember the key shortcuts: Win+Shift+S (Windows), Cmd+Shift+4 (Mac), and Power+Volume Down (Android) or Side+Volume Up (iPhone). Practice these and you'll never miss capturing something on your screen.
