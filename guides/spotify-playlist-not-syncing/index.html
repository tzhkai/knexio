Spotify Playlist Not Syncing?

Fix Spotify sync issues across devices

Quick Fix: Open your playlist on all devices simultaneously and play a song on each. This forces Spotify to sync playlist data immediately.

Why Your Spotify Playlist Isn't Syncing

Playlist sync issues on Spotify typically occur due to network problems, account conflicts, cached data corruption, or outdated app versions. Understanding the root cause helps you apply the right fix.

  • Poor internet connection — Spotify requires stable WiFi or mobile data to sync playlists across devices
  • Outdated Spotify app — Older versions may have sync bugs that were fixed in newer releases
  • Corrupted cache — Cached data can become corrupted and prevent proper sync
  • Offline mode enabled — If Offline mode is on, new playlist changes won't download
  • Account conflicts — Being logged into different accounts on different devices
  • Server issues — Spotify's servers may experience temporary sync delays

1. Check Your Internet Connection

Spotify requires internet to sync playlists. Make sure you're connected to WiFi or mobile data on all devices. Try opening a web page or streaming a video to confirm your connection is working. If you're on mobile data, ensure you haven't exceeded your data limit or hit Spotify's data restrictions.

2. Log Out and Log Back In

This is one of the most effective fixes for sync issues. It refreshes your account connection and clears any temporary authentication problems.

On iPhone/Android: Go to Settings → Your Profile Name → Sign Out. Then sign back in with your credentials.

On Desktop (Windows/Mac): Click your profile picture in the top-right corner → Settings → Scroll down to "Sign Out". Restart Spotify and log back in.

3. Enable Offline Mode (Then Disable)

This might seem counterintuitive, but toggling Offline mode forces Spotify to re-download and re-sync all playlist data. Here's how:

On Mobile: Go to Settings → Playback → Toggle "Offline" ON. Wait 10-15 seconds, then toggle it OFF. This forces Spotify to re-sync your playlists.

On Desktop: Go to Edit → Preferences → Scroll to "Offline" and toggle the setting on and off.

4. Force Sync by Playing a Song

Open the playlist on each device and play a song. This forces Spotify to sync the playlist data. The trick is to have the playlist open on all devices at the same time and play a track within 10-20 seconds on each device.

5. Update Spotify

Make sure Spotify is updated to the latest version on all devices. Old versions may have sync bugs.

On iPhone: Open App Store → Tap your profile → Scroll to see pending updates → Update Spotify

On Android: Open Google Play Store → Tap your profile → My apps → Update Spotify

On Desktop: Click Help → Check for Updates. If a new version is available, it will automatically download.

6. Clear Cache

This removes cached data and can fix sync problems. However, note that clearing cache will require you to re-download any offline music.

On Mobile: Settings → Storage → Clear Cache (or "Delete Cache" in some versions)

On Desktop: Edit → Preferences → Scroll down to "Storage" → Click "Clear Cache"

7. Check for Duplicate Accounts

Make sure you're using the same Spotify account on all devices. Check which account is currently logged in on each device:

On Mobile: Settings → View Profile → Make sure it shows your main account

On Desktop: Click your profile name in the top-right corner — the email shown should match your primary account

8. Check Spotify Server Status

Sometimes the issue is on Spotify's end. Check if there are any ongoing server issues:

  • Visit downdetector.com/status/spotify/ to see if other users are reporting problems
  • Check Spotify's official Twitter/X account for service announcements
  • Visit support.spotify.com for system status updates

9. Reinstall Spotify

If all else fails, completely removing and reinstalling Spotify often resolves persistent sync issues:

On Mobile: Long-press the Spotify app icon → Tap "Remove App" or go to Settings → General → iPhone Storage → Delete Spotify. Then reinstall from App Store.

On Desktop: Control Panel → Programs and Features → Uninstall Spotify. Download fresh installer from spotify.com and reinstall.

10. Create a New Playlist and Transfer

As a last resort, create a new playlist on your primary device and manually add your tracks. This effectively resets the playlist's sync state:

  • Create a new playlist on your main device
  • Add songs one by one or use "Select All" and "Add to Playlist"
  • Wait 2-3 minutes for the new playlist to sync
  • Delete the old playlist (optional, after confirming new one works)

Specific Error Messages and Fixes

"Sync Failed" or "Couldn't Sync"

This usually indicates a network issue. Try switching from WiFi to mobile data (or vice versa), or restart your router. Also ensure you're not connected through a VPN that Spotify might block.

"Playlist Unavailable" or Grayed Out Playlist

This can happen if the playlist was created by a deleted account or if there's a licensing issue. Try removing the playlist from your library and re-adding it. Go to the playlist → Click the three dots → Remove from Library, wait 30 seconds, then search for the playlist and add it again.

Songs Added But Not Showing Up

If songs appear in a playlist on one device but not another, try the "play a song on each device" method. If that doesn't work, go to the playlist settings and check if collaborative playlist is enabled — try toggling it off and back on.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my playlist only sync one way?

If changes only go from your phone to desktop but not vice versa, make sure both devices are running the same Spotify version. Check that your desktop is using the same account as your mobile device. Also verify that "Show local files" isn't causing conflicts.

Does Spotify Premium sync better than Free?

Both versions sync playlists equally well, but Premium users can download playlists for offline listening, which can help with sync reliability in areas with poor connectivity. Free users rely more on real-time streaming for sync.

How long does playlist sync take?

Normally, playlist changes appear within 10-30 seconds on other devices. If it's taking longer than 5 minutes, check your internet connection. Very large playlists with hundreds of songs may take longer to fully sync.

Can I force Spotify to sync immediately?

Yes — the most reliable method is to have the playlist open on all devices and play a song on each within 20 seconds. The playback action triggers an immediate sync attempt. You can also close and reopen Spotify on each device.

Why are my Liked Songs not syncing?

Liked Songs is essentially a playlist called "Your Music" or "Liked Songs." Follow the same troubleshooting steps as regular playlists. Make sure the "Liked Songs" section is showing in your library — go to Your Library → Playlists and ensure "Liked Songs" is visible.

Summary

Spotify playlist sync issues are almost always fixable. Start with the quick fix (play a song on each device simultaneously), then work through the steps: log out and back in, clear cache, update the app, and finally reinstall if needed. Most users find that logging out and back in resolves the issue.

If you continue experiencing sync problems after trying all these steps, contact Spotify Support through the app (Settings → Help & Support → Contact Support) or visit support.spotify.com.
