How to Clear Cache

Step-by-step for Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and Edge

Why Clear Cache? Clearing your browser cache removes stored temporary files that can cause issues like slow loading, outdated page versions, login problems, and website display errors.

What is Browser Cache?

Browser cache is a temporary storage location where your browser saves website data — images, scripts, stylesheets, and other resources — to speed up page loading on future visits. Instead of re-downloading everything each time you visit a site, your browser loads files from the cache.

While useful, cached data can become outdated or corrupted, leading to:

  • Websites not loading the latest version
  • Login errors or session problems
  • Display glitches and missing elements
  • Slow browsing performance
  • Inability to load new content

Google Chrome

Chrome is the most popular browser, and clearing its cache is straightforward.

Method 1: Keyboard Shortcut

Press Ctrl+Shift+Delete (Windows) or Cmd+Shift+Delete (Mac) to open the clear browsing data window directly.

Method 2: Through Settings

  1. Click the three dots (⋮) in the top-right corner
  2. Select "Settings"
  3. Click "Privacy and security" on the left sidebar
  4. Click "Clear browsing data"

Settings to Choose

  • Time range: Select "All time" to clear everything, or "Last hour" / "Last 24 hours" for less
  • Cached images and files: CHECK this (main cache to clear)
  • Cookies and other site data: CHECK if you're having login issues (will log you out of sites)
  • Browsing history: Optional — removes your visit history
  • Download history: Optional — won't delete actual downloaded files
  • Passwords: UNCHECK — clearing this removes saved passwords
  • Autofill form data: UNCHECK — keeps your saved addresses and info
💡 Quick Shortcut: Type chrome://settings/clearBrowserData in the address bar to go directly to the clear data page.

Mozilla Firefox

Firefox offers similar cache-clearing options with a few unique features.

Method 1: Keyboard Shortcut

Press Ctrl+Shift+Delete (Windows) or Cmd+Shift+Delete (Mac) to open the Clear Data window.

Method 2: Through Settings

  1. Click the hamburger menu (☰) in the top-right corner
  2. Select "Settings"
  3. Click "Privacy & Security" in the left sidebar
  4. Scroll to "Cookies and Site Data" and click "Clear Data"

Settings to Choose

  • Cached Web Content: CHECK this to clear the cache
  • Cookies and Site Data: CHECK if having login issues
  • Time range to clear: Select "Everything" for full clear
💡 Firefox Specific: You can set Firefox to automatically clear cache when closing. Go to Settings → Privacy & Security → Scroll to "Cookies and Site Data" → Check "Delete cookies and site data when Firefox is closed"

Safari (Mac)

Safari handles cache differently and offers several methods to clear it.

Method 1: Through Preferences

  1. Click "Safari" in the menu bar
  2. Select "Settings" (or "Preferences")
  3. Go to the "Advanced" tab
  4. Check "Show Develop menu in menu bar"
  5. Click the new "Develop" menu in the menu bar
  6. Select "Empty Caches" or press Cmd+Option+E

Method 2: Clear All Website Data

  1. Click "Safari" → "Settings"
  2. Go to "Privacy"
  3. Click "Manage Website Data"
  4. Click "Remove All" to clear everything, or select specific sites

Method 3: Clear History with Cache

  1. Click "Safari" → "Clear History"
  2. Choose a time range (e.g., "all history")
  3. Click "Clear History" — this clears both history and cache

Safari (iPhone / iPad)

iOS Safari has its own cache-clearing process separate from macOS Safari.

Steps to Clear Cache on iOS

  1. Open the Settings app
  2. Scroll down and tap "Safari"
  3. Scroll to the bottom and tap "Clear History and Website Data"
  4. Confirm by tapping "Clear History and Data"
💡 Note: On iOS, clearing Safari cache also clears cookies. You'll need to re-login to websites after clearing.

Microsoft Edge

Edge uses the same underlying engine as Chrome, so the process is very similar.

Method 1: Keyboard Shortcut

Press Ctrl+Shift+Delete to open the clear browsing data window.

Method 2: Through Settings

  1. Click the three dots (⋯) in the top-right corner
  2. Select "Settings"
  3. Click "Privacy, search, and services" in the left sidebar
  4. Click "Choose what to clear" under "Clear browsing data"

Settings to Choose

  • Time range: Select "All time" for complete clear
  • Cached images and files: CHECK this
  • Cookies and other site data: CHECK if having login issues
  • Browsing history: Optional
💡 Quick Shortcut: Type edge://settings/clearBrowserData in the address bar.

Google Chrome (Mobile)

On Android

  1. Open the Chrome app
  2. Tap the three dots in the top-right
  3. Tap "Settings"
  4. Tap "Privacy and security"
  5. Tap "Clear browsing data"
  6. Select "Cached images and files" (and "Cookies and site data" if needed)
  7. Tap "Clear data"

Quick Mobile Shortcut

Type chrome://settings/clearBrowserData in the address bar on mobile to go directly to the clear data screen.

What Gets Cleared When You Clear Cache?

Understanding what each option does helps you clean only what you need:

  • Cache/Cached images and files: Website assets stored locally (images, scripts, styles). Clearing this fixes display issues but may slow initial page loads temporarily.
  • Cookies: Small text files that remember login sessions, preferences, and tracking data. Clearing cookies logs you out of websites.
  • Browsing history: List of websites you've visited. Useful for privacy but not required for most troubleshooting.
  • Download history: Record of files you've downloaded. Does NOT delete the actual downloaded files from your computer.
  • Passwords: Saved login credentials. Only check this if you want to remove all saved passwords.
  • Autofill data: Saved addresses, phone numbers, and form entries. Uncheck to preserve this data.

When Should You Clear Cache?

Here are common scenarios when clearing cache is helpful:

  • Website not loading correctly — If a site looks broken, shows old content, or has missing images
  • Login problems — If you can't log in or keep getting logged out
  • Slow browsing — If pages are loading slower than usual
  • After website updates — If a website looks different but your browser shows the old version
  • Privacy concerns — If you want to remove stored tracking data
  • Troubleshooting — When debugging website issues, always start with a clean cache
💡 Pro Tip: When troubleshooting website issues, open the site in Incognito/Private mode. This uses a fresh cache, helping you determine if the issue is cache-related.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will clearing cache delete my passwords?

No. Clearing cache only removes temporary files (images, scripts, etc.). Passwords are stored separately — they are only deleted if you specifically check "Passwords" or "Save passwords" option during the clear process. Always verify what's selected before clicking "Clear data."

How often should I clear cache?

For most users, clearing cache every few months or when troubleshooting issues is sufficient. You don't need to clear it weekly — modern browsers manage cache efficiently. However, if you notice websites loading slowly, showing outdated content, or having display issues, it's time to clear.

What's the difference between cache and cookies?

Cache stores page files (images, scripts, stylesheets) for faster loading. It helps websites load quickly but can show outdated content. Cookies store login sessions, preferences, and tracking data. They're smaller text files that tell websites who you are and what settings you prefer.

Does clearing cache help with slow internet?

Clearing cache can help if the slowness is due to corrupted cached files or browser performance issues. However, if your internet connection itself is slow, clearing cache won't help. Check your internet speed and router if slowness persists across all websites.

Will clearing cache log me out of websites?

Only if you also clear "Cookies" or "Cookies and site data." Simply clearing "Cached images and files" won't log you out. However, many browsers combine these options, so check carefully before clearing if you want to stay logged in.

What's Incognito mode and does it use cache?

Incognito/Private mode doesn't save your browsing history, cookies, or cache to your device. Each incognito window starts fresh. However, websites can still track you through their own servers, and your ISP can still see what you visit.

Can I clear cache for just one website?

Yes. Most browsers allow you to clear data for specific sites. In Chrome: Settings → Privacy and security → Third-party cookies → See all site data → Find the specific site and click "Remove". In Safari: Preferences → Privacy → Manage Website Data → Select specific site → Remove.

Summary

Clearing browser cache is a fundamental troubleshooting step that fixes many common web browsing issues. The process varies slightly between browsers but follows the same general pattern: access settings, find privacy/clear data options, select what to clear, and confirm.

Remember: clearing cache is safe and doesn't delete your personal files or permanent settings. It's one of the first steps to try when experiencing website display issues, login problems, or slow browsing.
