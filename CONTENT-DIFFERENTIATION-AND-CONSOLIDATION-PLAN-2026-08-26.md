# Knexio content differentiation and consolidation plan

**Prepared:** 26 August 2026  
**Scope:** The current Workflow Library guide set, with emphasis on articles that share an “AI prompt + steps + review checklist” presentation pattern.  
**Decision status:** This is an editorial plan. It does **not** delete URLs, add redirects, or claim search-performance data.

## Executive judgment

The site has a coherent subject area, but several guides previously looked interchangeable because they followed the same surface formula while leaving their **input, output, decision boundary, and source record** implicit. The recommended response is not bulk deletion. It is to preserve pages that perform a distinct reader task, make that distinction visible in the opening, method, artifact, and internal links, and postpone any URL merge until real user/search evidence and editorial review support it.

The four flagship guides now have intentionally non-interchangeable jobs. The evidence matrix inspects **atomic claims**; the research brief compresses reviewed material for a **decision owner**; the priority plan chooses a **reversible next move**; and the meeting decision brief establishes the **status and confirmation path** for a choice discussed in a meeting.

| Flagship guide | Required input | Unique output | It must not replace |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence Matrix | Source register and atomic claim notes | Claim-level inspection table with support labels and verification work | A readable research brief, a decision, or a compliance finding |
| Research Brief | Reviewed source material plus a decision question | One-page decision-facing brief with sources, limits, and open questions | Claim-level source inspection or a commitment |
| Evidence to Priority Plan | Reviewed brief/matrix, constraints, and decision status | One reversible next move with a disconfirming condition and review gate | Weekly task planning, approval, or a promised delivery result |
| Meeting Notes to Decision Brief | Primary meeting record and stated authority | A Confirmed/Proposed/Deferred/Not confirmed decision record | Minutes, an action list, or consensus inferred from discussion |

## What to retain and how to differentiate it

| Cluster | Pages whose topic or template language overlaps | Recommended action | Editorial distinction that must remain visible |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research and decisions | Evidence Matrix, Research Brief, Decision Log, Decision Memo, Evidence to Priority Plan | **Retain all five; establish a strict sequence.** | Matrix = claim inspection; Brief = concise decision input; Log = durable record of an authorized choice; Memo = explanation to a reader; Priority Plan = reversible action proposal. |
| Meetings and follow-up | Meeting Agenda, Action List, Follow-up Email, Meeting Notes to Decision Brief, Minutes vs Decision Brief | **Retain all; clarify record type at the title and first screen.** | Agenda = prepare a conversation; Action List = stated commitments; Follow-up = request corrections; Decision Brief = choice status/rationale; comparison page = choose the correct record. |
| Planning and execution | Weekly Review, Weekly Priorities, 30-minute Starting Plan, Project Handoff, Evidence to Priority Plan | **Retain all; differentiate time horizon and decision state.** | Weekly Review = assess existing record; Weekly Priorities = allocate an approved focus; Starting Plan = first immediate action; Handoff = transfer continuity; Evidence to Priority = bridge reviewed evidence into a proposed/reversible move. |
| Writing and updates | Brief-first Pattern, Project Update, Decision Email | **Retain; add explicit audience and communication outcome.** | Brief-first = prompt construction; Project Update = status plus one ask; Decision Email = request or explain a defined choice. Neither is a source-validation process. |
| Research inputs | Customer Feedback Theme Map, Evidence Matrix, Research Brief | **Retain; prevent source-role confusion.** | Theme Map = organize repeated qualitative feedback; Matrix = inspect support for atomic claims; Brief = package reviewed findings for a decision. |

## Immediate implementation rules

Every retained guide should place its distinct job above the fold in four fields: **Use it when**, **Do not use it for**, the prompt’s required input, and the first internal-link module. Reusing a general “four steps” layout is acceptable only when the page also has a task-specific source/method block or a task-specific working asset. A generic prompt must not be the page’s only substantive value.

The flagship sequence should be linked as a reader journey rather than described as an SEO funnel:

1. Start with **Evidence Matrix** when the record contains multiple claims or source types that must be inspected.
2. Continue to **Research Brief** when the decision-critical rows can be compressed for a decision owner.
3. Use **Decision Log** or **Decision Memo** only after a real authority has selected an option and needs a durable record or a reader-facing explanation.
4. Use **Evidence to Priority Plan** when reviewed evidence supports a bounded next move but approval, dependency, or disconfirming evidence still matters.

The Meetings sequence should prevent a common failure mode: treating the most energetic discussion as a commitment. The agenda prepares the conversation, the action list captures only stated commitments, the decision brief sets decision status and rationale, and the follow-up asks participants to correct the record.

## Conditional consolidation candidates — do not act yet

There is currently insufficient evidence to delete or redirect any URL. The following pairs are the highest candidates for a later editorial review because their page structures may still overlap after differentiation. A merge should be considered only when one page has no distinct examples, no unique working artifact, no meaningful internal-link role, and no independent query/reader need after a review period.

| Candidate pair | Current difference worth preserving | Trigger for a later merge review | Safe action if approved later |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision Log ↔ Decision Memo | Internal durable record versus reader-facing explanation and request | One page cannot demonstrate a unique input/output or attracts no distinct internal journey | Consolidate content into the stronger canonical page, then create a genuinely equivalent 301 only after an approved URL map. |
| Meeting Notes to Action List ↔ Meeting Notes to Decision Brief | Commitments versus decision status/rationale | The decision brief ceases to have a separate source/confirmation record, or action list is expanded to duplicate it | Keep separate unless a rewrite removes the decision-status task; do not redirect by title similarity alone. |
| Weekly Priorities ↔ Evidence to Priority Plan | Capacity allocation for approved work versus evidence-to-decision bridge | The priority-plan page loses its evidence provenance, disconfirming condition, and review gate | Retain the bridge page while it maintains its source-to-plan role. |
| Brief-first Pattern ↔ Research Brief | Prompt framework versus evidence-backed decision document | Research Brief becomes a generic writing template without a source register | Retain; these solve different points in the workflow. |

## Editorial acceptance test for a retained URL

Before publishing or materially updating a guide, the editor should be able to complete this sentence without borrowing the same answer from another page:

> “A reader starts with **[specific input]**, uses this guide to create **[specific output]**, and must not use it when **[boundary]** applies.”

The page should then include one original asset, record, or decision table that a reader can copy; explicit human-review work; and at least two natural links to the preceding or following task. Where it uses an external example, the article must link the original public source and identify whether the example is a public-source walkthrough or an illustrative composite.

## Evidence and review boundary

This plan is based on the site’s current guide data and rendering structure, not on a claim that Google will treat any specific page as duplicate or thin content. It does not guarantee indexing, ranking, traffic, or AdSense approval. The next editorial review should re-check distinctness after the rewritten flagship pages are live and actual crawl/search data has had time to accumulate.
