Tips for YouTube Timestamps

Create chapter markers that actually work:

  • First Timestamp Must Be 0:00: YouTube requires the first chapter to start at 0:00 for automatic chapter detection to work. Without it, YouTube won't generate the chapter scrubber bar.
  • Each Line Needs Both Time and Description: YouTube parses each line as MM:SS - Description. The dash separator and description are both required — a bare timestamp with no label won't create a chapter.
  • Keep Descriptions Short: Long descriptions get cut off in the video player's chapter overlay. Aim for 2-5 words that identify the section clearly (e.g. "Introduction" not "In this section we will cover the background and context").
  • Chapters Must Be in Order: Timestamps must increase sequentially. A 5:00 chapter followed by a 3:00 chapter breaks the entire chapter list — YouTube ignores the out-of-order entry.

How YouTube Chapters Work

When you paste timestamps into a video description, YouTube automatically converts them into clickable chapters:

  • Automatic detection: YouTube scans the description for timestamp lines. If the first line starts at 0:00 and all timestamps are in ascending order, the video gets chapter markers on the progress bar.
  • Minimum requirements: You need at least 3 chapters (including 0:00), and each chapter must be at least 10 seconds long. Shorter chapters are ignored.
  • Clickable time links: Even without chapter detection, timestamps in the format MM:SS or HH:MM:SS become clickable links in the description. Viewers can jump directly to that moment.
  • SEO benefit: Well-structured chapters help YouTube understand your video's content, which can improve search rankings and surface your video in suggested clips.
