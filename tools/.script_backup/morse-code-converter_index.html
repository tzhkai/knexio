Frequently Asked Questions

What is Morse code?
Morse code is a communication system that encodes letters and numbers as sequences of short signals (dots, •) and long signals (dashes, −). It was invented by Samuel Morse and Alfred Vail in the 1830s and was widely used for telegraph communication.
How do you separate letters and words in Morse code?
Letters within a word are separated by a single space. Words are separated by a forward slash (/) or three spaces. For example: "HI THERE" = .... .. / - .... . .-. .
What is WPM (Words Per Minute) in Morse code?
WPM measures how fast Morse code is transmitted. 1 WPM is based on sending the word "PARIS" once per minute. Beginners start at 5–10 WPM; experienced operators reach 25–30 WPM.
What does SOS mean in Morse code?
SOS is the international distress signal: · · · − − − · · · (three dots, three dashes, three dots). Contrary to popular belief, SOS doesn't stand for any specific phrase — it was chosen because the pattern is simple and distinctive.
