About Time Zones

UTC vs GMT

UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) is the world's primary time standard. GMT is historically Greenwich Mean Time — they differ by less than 1 second and are used interchangeably for most purposes.

Daylight Saving Time

DST shifts clocks +1 hour in spring and −1 hour in autumn. Not all countries observe DST — notably China, Japan, and most of Africa do not change clocks.

IANA Timezone Names

Standard timezone IDs like "America/New_York" or "Asia/Tokyo" come from the IANA tz database and uniquely identify every zone including historical DST rules.

Remote Work Tip

For global teams, aim for a 2–3 hour overlap window. UTC 13–16 (1–4 PM) typically covers US morning (EST), US afternoon (PST), EU end of day, and early Asia morning.
